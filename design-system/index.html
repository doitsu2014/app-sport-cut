Overlay rule. Footage uses a fixed dark bed (#0A0F0C) and chalk text (#F2F5F0) with a 32–56% scrim. The overlay never samples the frame underneath it, so the score stays legible over a bright court or a dark hall.

05 · Components

Actions and inputs.

One primary action per pane. Labels are verb-first. Loading preserves the button's width; disabled controls keep their label and lose the pointer.

14 · Adoption

Rules, voice, and the floor.

The quality floor is accessibility and restraint. If a choice cannot be explained by the footage, the score, or the next action, remove it.

Do

  • checkKeep the video frame the brightest surface.
  • checkUse one primary action per pane, with a verb-first label.
  • checkLabel both score sides L and R; mark the server.
  • checkUse tabular figures for every score, duration, and timecode.
  • checkName the object in a destructive confirmation.
  • checkState the next action in an empty state or error.

Avoid

  • closeGradients, glows, glass panels, or celebratory animation.
  • closeCream, beige, and orange-brown page grounds.
  • closeA second accent-colored element in the same pane.
  • closeRed for a lost rally; danger is for failures only.
  • closeColor-only score states or unlabeled side markers.
  • closeInvented metrics, filler copy, or fake screenshots.

Accessibility floor

  • checkBody text reaches 4.5:1; large text and icons 3:1, in both themes.
  • checkEvery interactive element has a visible focus ring and an accessible name.
  • checkScores carry L/R labels; color never distinguishes sides or winners.
  • checkOverlay handles keep a 44px hit target even when drawn at 24px.
  • checkLayout survives 200% text scaling without clipping.
  • checkReduced motion removes transitions, not feedback.
  • checkToasts are never the only route to recovery.
  • checkNothing important sits under window chrome or platform overlays.
